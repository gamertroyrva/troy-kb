# Math Trade Business Pondering

## The Core Insight

The math trade generates an enormous amount of joy — and it does it for free. The joy already exists in board game communities; the math trade simply harvests it and hands it out. Like capturing nitrogen from air — the air never notices you grabbed some.

All the current tools are free: BGG, OLWLG, Google Suite, Discord. Which leads to the crazy sentence: **"there MUST be a way to monetize this."**

---

## The Opportunity as Troy Sees It

- Local no-ship math trades are generating real joy for a small slice of the board game community
- The vast majority of board game communities across the US have never experienced this
- The infrastructure cost to run one is essentially zero today
- There are untouched fields everywhere

**The local/scale distinction matters to Troy:** Monetizing *his RVA community* feels wrong. Bringing the joy to *communities that don't have it yet* feels different — that's expansion, not extraction.

---

## The Infrastructure Problem

OLWLG is the current tool. It works. Troy trusts it. But:

- It looks like it's straight out of the 1970s
- You cannot build a trustable business on the back of that old bag of bones
- A new system is needed

**Enter Ash** — NYC math trade organizer who piloted a new tool called **Atlas Realms** for a recent NYC No-Ship Math Trade. Troy participated. Troy gave Ash extensive verbal feedback by phone. Troy also sent Ash a follow-up email (drafted May 25) addressing Ash's question: *"What would it take for you to trust the system with your own group?"*

Troy's honest answer: the road to trust is long. OLWLG earned trust over 17+ years of real-world use and community-driven revision. Atlas Realms doesn't have that history yet. Accelerators could include visibility into what the system handles and what's still on the to-do list.

**Troy is watching Ash and Atlas Realms with interest.** Ash may be building the infrastructure Troy would need — or Troy may eventually need to build it himself or with others.

## The Open Questions (Not Yet Poked At Seriously)

- Who pays, and for what — and does that feel clean?
- What's the actual business model? Subscription? Per-trade fee? Community licensing?
- Is this a Troy-builds-it play, a Troy-partners-with-Ash play, or something else entirely?
- What would a real MVP look like?
