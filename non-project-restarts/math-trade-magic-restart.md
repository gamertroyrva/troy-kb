## The Core Idea: Crowdsourced Anomaly Detection via Full Transparency

**The William Way (status quo):** During practice runs, the organizer shares only a list of participants who would have received one or more trades. No trade details. No loops. No visibility into what traded for what.

**Troy's Way (used in local no-ship trades):** Troy shares the full OLWLG results summary during practice runs — every trade loop, every participant, every game given and received, all visible to everyone.

**The insight:** If Troy shares full results with the Eastern USA shipping math trade participants, the community itself becomes the anomaly detector. People will see their own trades, recognize mistakes, and flag them — no Block 3 required for this purpose.

---

## Why This Matters Beyond Anomaly Detection

Troy's local community *loves* seeing the full practice results. It turns math trade week into a community event. People are engaged, excited, invested. The loops are visible and beautiful.

The JK Riki blog post on "unfair trades" (Nov 2025) and its comment thread revealed something important: a participant named Matthew D has lost the magic of math trading entirely. He believes all lopsided results are products of mistakes — that there is no other source of magic. Troy believes this wound is real and consequential. Matthew D may not be alone.

Troy's full transparency approach -- showing practice results following practice runs — has the potential prevent the formation of additional Matthew D's -- people who get burned or see people get burned and then become jaded about whether there's any magic at all in a math trade.

**This reframes the idea from "anomaly detection alternative" to something bigger: magic preservation at scale, and harm prevention.**

The harm prevention piece is serious. When mistakes ride undetected through practice runs into final results, people get hurt. Devastatingly. Sometimes irreversibly. (Accounts deleted. Scandals. People never math trading again.) Full transparency during practice runs makes those mistakes visible and fixable before it's too late.

---

## The Tradeoff

**Option A (Status Quo):** Keep practice results binary (who got a trade / who didn't). Maximize theoretical trade numbers in the final run. Accept that preventable, soul-crushing mistakes may march straight through to final results undetected.

**Option B (Full Transparency):** Share complete practice results with all participants. Near-certain improvement in mistake detection. Possible reduction in final run trade numbers if participants use the information to avoid inconvenient trades.

**The precedent concern:** Matthew D cited Board Game Brawl (BGB), a trade that shared full results and saw final runs shrink smaller than practice runs — described as "almost pointless." However, BGB ultimately dissolved entirely, suggesting confounding factors beyond the transparency policy. This is a data point of one with significant uncertainty.
