## The Story, Chronologically

Troy's AI learning journey began on **Sunday, April 26, 2026** — not with a plan, but with a decision to stop watching AI unfold on LinkedIn from the sidelines and start acting. Day one alone: a Claude free trial started, and two agents already built in n8n. That's the tone the whole era would take — learn by doing real things, not toy examples.

The first two weeks were exploratory and a little chaotic in a good way. Troy cleaned up his own digital life (fixing his iPhone's name across Apple ID, clearing a hundred iCloud attachments), set up his information diet (Overcast, Substack, dropping phone games), and got his first taste of agentic behavior landing in the real world — a text from an agent on April 30 that he flagged, unprompted, as "so freaking amazing." By May 2, a "legendary" check-in with Michael Matherly accelerated things further, and Troy had his first stab at a math trade results visualization in Chat, plus his introduction to memory, Mem, and the connectors-vs-computer-use distinction.

May 1 marked the quiet start of what would become a defining structural choice: Cowork folder structure and global instructions, and an analysis of the Math_Trade folder — the first sign that Troy's actual hobbies (not hypotheticals) were going to be the proving ground for everything. That proving ground crystallized fast. Block 1 of what would become Math Trade Genie (standardized loops TXT) landed May 6; Block 2 (HTML visualization) was "crushed" the very next day. By May 8, Troy was already running a real organizer's OLWLG data through the pipeline and sharing results — the tool was live before the month was half over.

Mid-May brought the infrastructure that would carry the rest of the year: Python installed, Windows Terminal, the Anthropic API wired up (May 11); GitHub and Netlify accounts (May 12); PowerShell shortcuts (May 13). May 14 — Troy's wedding anniversary — was also the day he revised and tested the about-me.md context-file network that this very digest extends. May 15 brought two threads that would come to dominate the summer: scoping the MisoCon26 Attendee Tracker, and a "life-altering phone call with Andrew S." — the first contact with the man who would become Troy's first real consulting-style client.

The back half of May was a acceleration point. May 22 brought Obsidian, git, and a knowledge-base repo — Troy building his own memory infrastructure outside of Claude, in parallel with learning to work with Claude. May 25: the MisoCon tracker went from "not-yet-viable" to working, the same day Asher Atlas mentioned he was looking for a design partner. May 26 confirmed it: Ash said he wanted Troy specifically, and the same day, the mt_genie script was created and relocated to GitHub, immediately put to use on a real math trade run. May 28 was "the first day crushing it with Claude Code" — the tool that would become Troy's primary building environment for the rest of the year, a shift from Cowork that the philosophy section of about-me.md now reflects as settled preference.

June opened with ADS (Andrew's Driving Services) becoming a real, structured project — the Locations Data Dictionary completed June 5, RowBuilder 1.0 operational and "like the Death Star" by June 6, a demo to Andrew on June 8 that Troy called hitting it out of the park. In parallel, the Math Trade Organizer Playbook took shape (sections built June 13–14, the Automation Opportunity Matrix planned June 15, Phases 1–3 completed and published to a claude.ai webpage by June 24). June also carried real workplace friction — Troy spent days in mid-June documenting "AI adoption friction points" at Markel, calling the resulting list "a powder keg," escalating it through his manager and networking with Arthur Kingdom, someone he'd keep in orbit through the summer.

July was defined as much by community as by code. The RVA math trade participant tracker was built and then transformed into a real engagement tool (July 1). But July 11 brought real loss: Blake Morris, a close friend in the Richmond board gaming community, passed away. Troy's response over the following weeks was consistent with who he is — he built a "Games I Have Played with Blake Morris" GeekList the same day, made a Discord announcement, drafted and sent communication to everyone on what he started calling "the Blake List," and began planning what would become a memorial event. The rest of July's technical work (swap day artifacts, Claude Code specs for processing rules, the "Richmond's Most Unwanted" narrative project — Troy's playful side showing through with a self-coined term, "rap sheet") ran alongside that grief, not separate from it.

August was the month the agentic loop finally worked. After a rough first attempt (August 6: "didn't get far") and a week of rebuilding loop artifacts, settings.json, and agent MD files, August 8 delivered the actual breakthrough: "Agentic AI populated a beautiful row of ADS master, huge milestone." From there it was rungs — a ladder of increasingly ambitious autonomous runs, each one debugged and pushed further: Rung 2's quagmires (Aug 12) fixed into a clean 3-row run by Aug 13; a 10-row run with a business-analyst agent by Aug 14; a 25-row goal that finished 24 rows before needing input on Aug 16; another 25 rows the next day. Rung 8A was bumpy (Aug 20, "so much learning"), a travel break followed, and then — after infra changes and Rung 9 — August 30 delivered the payoff for the entire summer's work: "ADS Master complete — all rows filled in. Are you kidding me?" It was formatted and sent to Andrew Sawyer on August 31, closing this era cleanly on the exact date this digest ends.

Threaded through all of it, quietly: A Game Day for Blake was taking shape in the background (Eventbrite created Aug 25, invitations sent Aug 26 with strong response), and toward the very end of this window, a heavier note entered the record — Troy's mother Marion's cancer diagnosis surfaced on August 27, with a staging update (Stage IVa) noted August 29, right in the same week as the ADS Master finish line. Two very different kinds of milestones landing in the same handful of days.
