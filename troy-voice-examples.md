## Distilled Rules for Composing Outbound Communications in Troy's Structure and Voice

1. **Lead with the recipient's own specific detail back at them.** Show you heard them before you say anything about yourself.
2. **Use a closing question to invite action rather than assuming it.** "Does that sound like a good way to get plugged in?" beats "Hope to see you there."
3. **Sign off in your natural community vernacular.** "Hit me up," "Happy trading," "Open-mindedly" — the sign-off is relational, not formal.
4. **Reference prior conversations when they exist.** Frame proposals as continuations of shared thinking, not new asks landing cold.
5. **Acknowledge competing priorities honestly rather than glossing over them.** "Gotta focus on that" is more Troy than pretending the slate is clear.
6. **Don't withhold useful context to be coy — share it naturally.** If the dates matter, give them. The email is better for it.
7. **Closings should reflect the specific relationship and moment.** Match the warmth and register to where the relationship actually is.
8. **Troy's register is community, not corporate.** "Here's what I'm thinking..." is Troy. "That exercise is going to teach me things" is AI. When in doubt, go casual and direct over polished and declarative.

## Example 1: BGG Math Trade Discussion Thread Post
*Context: Troy responding to Wisconsin/Chicago gamers who advocated for inclusion in the Second East Coast Math Trade. Posted May 2026.*

---

Hello everyone!  Didn't jump into this discussion right away because I had to consider all the points being made, plus my own thoughts.  I'm hearing a few devastating arguments.  One, Wisconsin and Illinois/Chicago are closer to the population centers already in the trade than is Florida.  Stuff like that…not gonna get too mathematical. Two, move the line, do a trade, next group asks for line to be moved…gotta just pick something and stick with it.  Three, "having less shipping cost" math trades…anybody can organize them.  Well, anybody with time and patience.

Where I land is with an overwhelming desire to stick with what I communicated.  While at the same time being open-minded about a geographical reformulation for any next shipping trade that I organize.  Who knows if I'll organize another one…I don't see why I wouldn't.  I could be willing to run an Upper Midwest shipping Math Trade.  Especially if somebody is willing to "own the boundary".  (is there any rule that says a person living in Virginia is not allowed to organize a math trade for others outside their region?)  I love running math trades.  Boundary formation, that's somewhat interesting, but it's not as cool as the math trades themselves.
