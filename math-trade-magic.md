## What a Math Trade Is

A math trade is a large-scale multi-party trade event, typically organized
through OLWLG (Online Want List Generator) and the BoardGameGeek community.
Participants list games they are willing to trade away and games they want to
receive. An algorithm finds a set of trades that maximizes the number of
participants who get something they want. Nobody trades directly with anyone
else — trades happen in loops. A gives to B, B gives to C, C gives to A.
Everyone gets something. Nobody needs a perfect one-for-one match.

Troy has organized local no-ship math trades for the Richmond, Virginia board
gaming community twice per year since 2023. The May 2026 Eastern USA shipping
math trade — approximately 170 participants trading games across the eastern
United States — is his first regional shipping trade, and whether it becomes
a recurring event depends on how this one goes.

## The Source of Math Trade Magic

Here is the thing that not everyone understands, and that a smaller subset
*truly* understands:

The happiness already exists. It is latent — sitting in collections,
trapped in games that are owned by the wrong people. A game that brings one
person zero joy might be the exact game another person has been hunting for
months. The math trade does not create value. It *extracts* it. Like pulling
nitrogen from the air — the nitrogen was always there, abundant, just
unavailable without the right mechanism.

The organizer's reward is not a game. It is watching the nitrogen get
harvested.

## The Magnitude of the Magic

This is where it gets staggering.

Even in a tiny practice run — 8 trades, 2 loops — the anomaly detector
surfaced a $25 value gap and a $20 value gap, both completely legitimate.
Both participants knew exactly what they were doing. Both were extracting
personal value that market value cannot measure.

Now scale that. A real math trade run for 170 participants might produce
500 or more individual trades. Each one represents a participant shedding
something they are done with and receiving something they genuinely want.
The aggregate happiness being harvested across those 500 trades is not
$25 here and $20 there. It is enormous. It is the whole point.

The tools Troy is building exist to make sure that mechanism runs cleanly
at scale — that nobody gets hurt accidentally while all that happiness is
being extracted.

## Market Value vs. Personal Value

The Math Trade Genie's anomaly detector compares trades using market value
as a proxy. This is useful but imperfect — and Troy knows exactly why.

Consider: a participant who had a miserable experience playing a game with
friends may value it at less than zero. It is taking up shelf space and
carrying bad memories. Trading it away for a game he actually wants is
a fantastic deal by any personal metric, regardless of what the market says.

The anomaly detector is not a judge. It is a safeguard. Its job is to flag
trades where the market value gap is large enough to warrant a human look —
not because the participant is being cheated, but because something might
have gone wrong accidentally. A mislisted item. A new participant who didn't
fully understand the process.

When the detector flags a trade, the question is never "should I fix this?"
It is "should I look at this?" Almost always, the answer after looking is:
the participant knows exactly what they're doing. Leave it alone.
